# 经济商业类专项训练

Passage 1
 
It seems obvious that you don’t give away your product for free but this is exactly what indie rock group The Crimea did earlier this year\. The band’s reasoning goes like this: more people will download the free album than would pay for it\. Therefore, more people will hear the Crimea’s music\. These people will then pay money for concerts by the band and perhaps buy a T\-shirt or other merchandise\. If the band play regular concerts to crowds of 200 or 300 people they can make more money than they would from sales of CDs\. There will always be some people who want something they can hold in their hands so they will release the CD into the shops too—but making money through sales of their music isn’t the top priority\.
 
The story illustrates the creative thinking going on in the music business in response to dramatic changes over the last few years in the way that people buy music\. Sales of music digitally—to computer, MP3 players and mobile phones—reached nearly 52 billion dollars in 2006—an increase of almost 100% on the previous year\. People are simply not buying CDs in the way they used to, and by the time they stop buying them entirely, the music industry wants to make sure they are buying music in another form\.
 
This trend looks set to continue so the big question for the music industry is whether they can successfully manage the move to being primarily a digital industry without profits falling to unacceptable levels\.
 
There are both positive and negative signs\. On the plus side, more and more people are buying music online\. At present, teenagers buy a lot of music on mobile phones\. Research by UK mobile operators suggests that 75 percent of 16\- to 24\-year\-olds want to buy a track they like if they hear it played on the radio\. But with so much competition, it is impossible for all bands to make an impact immediately by doing a free download\.
 
The bad news for the record companies, however, is the opportunity to illegally download music\. Piracy—usually in the form of cheaply copied CDs—has long been a problem for the music business but the internet is allowing pirates to distribute freely and broadly through file\-sharing sites and large pirate download sites\.
 
In this situation there is a danger that bands may give away their music for free and promote it in the next few years, a very interesting thing to start observing in the music business\.
